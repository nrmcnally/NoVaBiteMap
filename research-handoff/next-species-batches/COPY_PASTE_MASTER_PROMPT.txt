RESEARCH QUESTION

For each species in the active batch, determine what scientific evidence exists
for predicting:

1. Hook-and-line or angler catchability
2. Feeding rate or prey consumption
3. Foraging, attack, or prey-selection behavior
4. Movement or general activity
5. Habitat occupancy
6. Growth, physiology, stress, or survival

Keep these endpoints separate.

Evidence measuring endpoints 1–3 may support BiteMap scoring rules.

Evidence measuring endpoints 4–6 is normally contextual and must not become a
bite-score rule unless the study directly connects the measurement to feeding or
catchability.

Specifically:

- Growth optimum is not automatically bite optimum.
- Thermal preference is not automatically feeding optimum.
- Movement is not automatically feeding.
- Habitat presence is not automatically catchability.
- Creel seasonality can be confounded by angler effort, regulations, fish
  abundance, and access.
- Post-stocking accessibility is not automatically a feeding surge.
- Spawning-related catches may reflect aggression or nest defense rather than
  feeding.

VARIABLES TO INVESTIGATE

Search for species-specific evidence concerning:

- Absolute water temperature
- Thermal acclimation
- Seasonal temperature
- Short-term temperature trend and rate of temperature change
- Extreme heat and cold
- Dissolved oxygen in mg/L and oxygen saturation percentage
- Temperature and oxygen interactions
- Time of day, diel cycles, underwater light, and photoperiod
- Cloud cover, turbidity, and water clarity
- Spawning stage, migration, nest defense, and post-spawn behavior
- Streamflow, discharge, rate of flow change, flooding, and current velocity
- Tidal stage, tidal current, and salinity
- Barometric pressure and weather fronts
- Rainfall, wind, and moon phase
- Prey or forage availability
- Recent stocking
- Angling pressure, hook avoidance, learning, and depletion
- Fish size, life stage, sex, population, strain, and ecotype

Barometric pressure, fronts, moon phase, and similar weather variables must not
be scored merely because anglers commonly discuss them. Require direct,
species-specific evidence measuring feeding, foraging, or catchability.

Bait, lure, hook, gear, and presentation findings may be documented when
supported by controlled experiments or reliable creel evidence. These usually
belong in the Fish Guide rather than the environmental bite score.

ADVERSARIAL REVIEW

After drafting each species report, conduct a hostile second-pass review.

Challenge every proposed score rule:

- Was feeding or catchability actually measured?
- Is this merely movement, habitat use, growth, or survival?
- Is the life stage applicable?
- Was the research performed in aquaculture conditions?
- Is the geography transferable to Northern Virginia?
- Could effort, abundance, season, regulations, or gear explain the result?
- Is the rule double-counting temperature, season, oxygen, spawn, or flow?
- Does another study contradict it?
- Is an exact number being asserted beyond what the study measured?
- Is the source locator precise enough for another reviewer to reproduce the
  claim?

Preserve contradictory and null findings. Do not discard them to make the model
appear cleaner.
